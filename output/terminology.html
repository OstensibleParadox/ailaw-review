01 · How the canon binds

The file is the authority, not the prose.

Coining a term is legitimate, and this Article does a great deal of it. The failure mode is not coining. It is coining where a standard term already exists, or coining and then drifting. Every entry below is classified on that axis.

A terminology question is settled by editing TERMINOLOGY.yaml and running the linter, never by editing a sentence. That is what keeps an agent, a co-author and a cite-checker working from the same vocabulary six months from now.

anchored · 3

A standard scholarly or statutory term already exists and is adopted. Use it exactly; do not invent a synonym.

coined · 39

The Article's own term for a concept with no standard name. Keep it, define it once, and never vary the wording.

borrowed · 2

Imported from an adjacent literature. Cite the source at first use and do not silently rename what that literature already named.

02 · Work no linter can do

18 required actions, 15 advisory.

String substitution is the easy half. These are the items where a term is lexically fine but sits on top of an established meaning in a neighbouring field, or where the draft assigns one object two owners. Each needs a sentence or a footnote, not a find-and-replace.

control_laneAnchor to the standard term

Thirteen uses of a load-bearing metaphor that is never formally defined. Define once as 'a risk-specific control allocation (a "control lane")' and the shorthand becomes licensed.

control_sheetInternal inconsistency

Recaption Table 3 'The Deployment-Record Control Sheet', or state the identity in Part IV.A.

de_minimisAnchor to the standard term

'de minimis' is a Latin adjectival phrase. 'non-de-minimally' is a manufactured adverb that will draw an editor query at any law review, and the standard formulation is available and current: 'bears more than a de minimis relation to'. Rewrite all five occurrences; do not merely make the two spellings agree.

Groff v. DeJoy, 600 U.S. 447, 468-71 (2023) (construing the de minimis formulation in Title VII accommodation)

deployment_control_cascadeAnchor to the standard term

The source literature calls this object a hierarchical control structure, with controllers, control actions, process models and feedback. Renaming it a 'cascade' without saying so creates a house term where a standard one exists, and obscures that the Article is adapting a recognised safety-engineering method rather than inventing a diagram.

Either rename to 'deployment control structure', or keep 'cascade' and add one sentence at the definition: 'The cascade adapts the hierarchical control structure of systems-theoretic accident analysis, retaining its controller, control-action and feedback vocabulary while using it to specify evidence rather than to assess safety.' The Article already cites Leveson; only the naming relationship is unstated.

Nancy G. Leveson, Engineering a Safer World 75-76, 87-90 (2012); Nancy G. Leveson & John P. Thomas, STPA Handbook 14-15 (2018)

deployment_recordThree names, one object

One object currently carries three names — 'local record' (§ 2), 'control sheet' (Part IV.A) and 'manifest' (Part IV.B, with closed/local/minimum qualifiers) — and no passage equates them. This is the most damaging terminology defect in the draft because it makes the central obligation look like three different duties.

Collapse to TWO names with a clean division of labour: 'deployment record' = the obligation (statutory register); 'control sheet' = the instrument that records it (document register). Delete 'manifest' entirely. Replace § 2's 'a local record' with 'a deployment record, maintained locally'.

deployment_record_dutyDefined twice

Marked with \term twice: once in the Introduction and again in Part II.D.

Keep the Part II.D definition, which sits adjacent to Model Act § 2. Demote the Introduction occurrence to an ordinary mention without \\term.

designationEnumeration differs across the draft

The enumerated content of a designation differs in three places. The Introduction lists seven items including 'trigger' but omitting the material-change threshold; Part II.A lists seven with 'claimant threshold' replacing 'trigger' and adding material-change rules; Model Act § 1 lists ten with neither 'trigger' nor 'claimant threshold' and adds affected persons, administering authority, enforcement route and civil-penalty ceiling.

Model Act § 1 is authoritative. Rewrite the other two to a strict subset of it, or mark them as illustrative with 'among other elements'.

gatewayAnchor to the standard term

Nine uses, no definition. Add a parenthetical where visible operator is defined: '(its gateway role)'. Then 'gateway' is a licensed shorthand rather than a floating synonym.

high_impactAnchor to the standard term

State once why the Article says high-IMPACT and not high-RISK. The reason is already in Part II.A — coverage follows the deployment, not the model — and it is a genuine contribution, but a reader who works from the EU or Colorado vocabulary will otherwise read the substitution as carelessness. Note also that 'high-impact system' was Canada's AIDA term, which supplies precedent rather than novelty.

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 arts. 6, 26 and Annex III ('high-risk AI system'); Colo. SB 24-205 ('high-risk artificial intelligence system'), repealed and replaced by SB 26-189 (eff. Jan. 1, 2027) (recast as 'automated decision-making technology'); Bill C-27, Artificial Intelligence and Data Act ('high-impact system') (Can.) (died on prorogation, 2025)

material_change_thresholdAssigned to two different actors

Set by the designation in three places (Part II.A, § 1, § 2's closing sentence) and by the operator's control sheet in three others (Part IV.A prose, Table 3, and the learning-systems paragraph). A reader cannot tell whether the threshold is statutory or self-set.

Designation sets it; the control sheet applies it. Amend Part IV.A to say the sheet applies the designation's material-change rule to this deployment. Both § 1 and § 2's closing sentence already support that reading, so only Part IV changes.

material_controlCollides with an established term

'Control' is a term of art in discovery, and the Article's own argument turns on the two not being the same thing. Part II.G distinguishes them, but the distinction arrives long after the term is introduced.

Footnote at FIRST use of 'material control': this test allocates information duties under the Act and neither states nor borrows the Rule 34 standard; § 2's retrieval right is what interacts with Rule 34, and a federal court decides possession, custody or control independently.

Fed. R. Civ. P. 34(a)(1); In re Citric Acid Litig., 191 F.3d 1090, 1107-08 (9th Cir. 1999) (legal-right test); Sergeeva v. Tripleton Int'l Ltd., 834 F.3d 1194, 1200-02 (11th Cir. 2016) (practical-ability approach)

opacity_trioCollides with an established term

Burrell's three forms (intentional secrecy, technical illiteracy, and the mismatch between machine optimisation and human semantic reasoning) are the canonical taxonomy in this literature and are DIFFERENT from these three. Presenting an unattributed second tripartite taxonomy invites the reading that the Article is restating Burrell inaccurately.

One footnote at first use: cite Burrell, state that her axis is the source of opacity while this axis is the locus of the missing fact, and that the two classifications are orthogonal.

Jenna Burrell, How the Machine 'Thinks': Understanding Opacity in Machine Learning Algorithms, 3 Big Data & Soc'y 1 (2016)

practical_authorityCollides with an established term

Near-identical wording for a different object: practical ability concerns obtaining documents, practical authority concerns controlling a deployment. The Article uses 'practical ability' in the Rule 34 passage and 'practical authority' in § 5, one Part apart, with no signal that the resemblance is not an equation.

Half-sentence at § 5(b)'s first prose appearance distinguishing the two.

Sergeeva v. Tripleton Int'l Ltd., 834 F.3d 1194, 1200-02 (11th Cir. 2016)

safe_exitCollides with an established term

A safe harbor confers immunity. Safe exit confers nothing of the kind: it ends a procedural role and leaves every merits element, defense and remedy untouched. Given that § 8's merits firewall does the real work of denying inferences, a reader who assimilates safe exit to safe harbor will misread the Article's central concession as far broader than it is.

Add to the definition sentence: 'Safe exit terminates the front-door role. It is not a safe harbor and confers no immunity from any underlying claim.'

shadow_treeManuscript hygiene

ailaw/source/*.tex is byte-identical to article/*.tex and nothing includes it. An edit made there will silently not compile. Delete it, or replace it with a symlink.

table_cross_referencesManuscript hygiene

Tables 1-3 carry \label but nothing in the text uses \ref, and the strings 'Table 1', 'Table 2', 'Table 3' never appear in the prose. A reader has no cue to look at them. Add one pointer sentence in each table's section.

verified_noticeAnchor to the standard term

§ 3 says 'a sworn declaration or declaration under penalty of perjury' (itself a redundant doublet — 'a declaration under penalty of perjury' suffices) while the prose says verified notice, front-door notice and compliant request. Introduce the short name in § 3 — 'such declaration, a "verified notice"' — and use it everywhere after.

visible_operatorAnchor to the standard term

State once why this is NOT 'deployer'. 'Deployer' selects on deployment role; 'visible operator' selects on visibility to the affected person, which is the whole point of a gateway the claimant can find. Without this sentence the term reads as an idiosyncratic synonym for the emerging standard term, which is the single most likely terminology objection from a reviewer who works in this area.

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 art. 3(4) ('deployer'); Colo. Rev. Stat. tit. 6, art. 1, pt. 17 (SB 24-205) ('deployer'), repealed and replaced by SB 26-189 (signed May 14, 2026, eff. Jan. 1, 2027), which retains the developer/deployer distinction

front_doorCollides with an established term

In technology law 'front door' already denotes a deliberately engineered lawful-access channel into an encrypted system, as opposed to a 'back door'. A substantial part of this Article's readership will hear that meaning first.

One footnote at first use disclaiming the encryption sense: this front door creates no access to system internals, no decryption obligation, and no law-enforcement channel.

Harold Abelson et al., Keys Under Doormats, 1 J. Cybersecurity 69 (2015)

information_materialityHeading does not match the term

The heading names two prongs by a bare noun and one by its full term. Asymmetric with the three \term names.

merits_firewallAnchor to the standard term

Restatement (Third) of Torts: Liability for Physical and Emotional Harm § 16 (2010); Fed. R. Evid. 407

pre_merits_attribution_gapAnchor to the standard term

The Article currently occupies this category without naming it, which costs it a recognised home in the literature.
