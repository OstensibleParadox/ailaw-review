# Revision notes: Part V.C and Part II

## Part V.C

- Recast the medical section around the protected function of professional judgment rather than a generic requirement for a human in the loop.
- Added *The Clinician's Veto* as a survey of 136 U.S. prescribing clinicians, not an interview study and not a physicians-only sample.
- Preserved the study's three-part architecture: action-gated calibrated confidence, differentiated epistemic/aleatoric communication, and decision-time inferential transparency.
- Stated the empirical result narrowly: the features are acceptance conditions, not proof of a legal standard of care or legal liability; baseline support for autonomous prescribing remained low.
- Made the Article's extension explicit: model-level uncertainty does not automatically become a system-level property in a retrieval/tool/subagent pipeline, so the legally relevant demand attaches to the deployed harness and its control path.
- Added the Utah Doctronic pilot as a concrete anti-substitution example. The text distinguishes the agreement's planned staged reduction of prospective review from the pilot's current status. Utah's current materials report that the pilot remains in Phase 1 and that every renewal still requires authorization by a licensed medical practitioner.
- Retained the relational-AI portion of the subsection.

## Part II

- Split the existing paragraph on legislation creating a limited AI-related platform.
- Added H.R. 238 as the live example: the bill would include qualifying AI/ML technology within the FFDCA phrase "practitioner licensed by law to administer such drug," while leaving the incidents and remedial architecture of that status unspecified.
- Preserved the distinction between authorizing an automatic instrumentality to perform a bounded function and creating a general artificial person.
