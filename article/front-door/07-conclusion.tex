\section*{Conclusion}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

AI litigation can fail before a court reaches duty, defect, discrimination, causation, immunity, or defense. The affected person sees a result; the identity, version, configuration, evaluation, incident, and stop records remain divided among organizations. Ordinary procedure becomes powerful once the proper target and record holder are known. It cannot guarantee those predicates or create a deployment record after the event.

The deployment front door fills that gap. For a legislatively designated high-impact deployment, a claimant with a legally cognizable injury or existing equitable entitlement and a plausible, risk-specific nexus may serve the visible operator. The operator identifies the system and relevant entities, preserves the record, and supplies a staged first answer. Only an enterprise with risk-specific, practical, and material control remains in the control lane; an enterprise without it completes the answer and terminates its special role.

The sequence is concrete. Legislatures designate the deployment and protected interest. Operators create a versioned minimum record and retrieval path before use. An affected person serves a verified, event-specific request on the entity already visible. The first answer maps identity, provenance, custody, and intervention authority. A cumulative control test confines participation to risk-specific, practically exercisable, material lanes. Federal procedure then governs any federal action. Each step solves one information problem and stops where the next body of law begins.

The rule works because record infrastructure precedes litigation. A versioned responsibility charter assigns risk and release ownership, feedback, intervention, provenance, and retention. The cascade specifies the facts relevant to authorization, notice, feasibility, causal sequence, and rebuttal. Privacy, privilege, trade secrets, security, proportionality, and federal procedure govern access. Compliance establishes no underlying breach, defect, causation, damages, or joint liability.

That sequence preserves the most valuable feature of ordinary law: its ability to distinguish cases. \emph{Garcia} shows why an integrated operator's internal record matters even when its name is obvious. \emph{Mobley} shows how a vendor-hosted, customer-controlled information structure can fracture identification and production across a divided stack. The front door makes the relevant facts reachable in both settings; the cause of action still determines who wins.

The payoff is larger than disclosure. A claimant can name the operative actor before limitations expire. A court can protect trade secrets while testing the relevant control. A supplier with no event or authority can leave early. An operator can retrieve a defined record instead of reconstructing an organization after harm. And a defendant can prove an inactive feature, independent modification, effective intervention, or unavailable alternative on the same record. Observability strengthens adjudication in both directions.

Deployment law is uniquely positioned to create that observability. It operates when provenance, versioning, authority, feedback, and retention can still be designed into the system. Procedure is uniquely positioned to protect and test the resulting record once a dispute begins. The front door connects those institutional strengths: ex ante law keeps the facts knowable, and adjudication assigns their legal meaning after the parties can see them.

That division remains durable as models, vendors, and deployment architectures change. Sectoral lawmakers can revise the minimum fields and retention clock without reopening the merits standard. Courts can apply familiar pleading, discovery, evidence, and liability rules to a record that identifies the actual system before them.

Probabilistic output does not eliminate legal responsibility; it relocates the evidence of control. The deployment front door makes that evidence reachable before information asymmetry decides the case.
