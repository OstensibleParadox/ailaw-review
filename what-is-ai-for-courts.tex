\title{Before the Merits: A Deployment Front Door for High-Impact AI Litigation}
\author{Anonymous}
\date{August 28, 2026}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
AI litigation can stall before substantive doctrine can operate. A claimant may observe an output or decision while the facts needed to identify the responsible actor---the model and application versions, system instructions, tool permissions, evaluations, complaints, escalation decisions, and stop authority---are divided among enterprises and unavailable until discovery. Discovery, however, ordinarily presupposes an identifiable party or nonparty target and cannot produce a risk record that the deployment never created.

This Article identifies that pre-merits attribution gap and proposes a deployment front-door rule. For a statutorily designated high-impact deployment, a claimant who alleges a legally cognizable injury or existing equitable entitlement and a plausible, risk-specific nexus may serve the visible operator. The operator must identify the deployed system and relevant entities, preserve the corresponding record, and provide staged, proportionate, nonprivileged access. An upstream or downstream enterprise enters the same process only when it possessed practical authority materially connected to the asserted risk. A merits firewall prevents participation from supplying any presumption of breach, defect, causation, damages, or joint liability; an enterprise without material control completes the first answer and terminates its special front-door role.

An ex ante deployment-record duty makes the rule usable by assigning risk ownership, approval, escalation, versioning, incident retention, and stop authority before harm occurs. A deployment-control cascade specifies the records relevant to authorization, notice, feasibility, causal sequence, and defense. Applications to \emph{Garcia v. Character Technologies} and \emph{Mobley v. Workday} show how the same rule opens an integrated consumer deployment and a divided employment stack while leaving ordinary law to decide who wins.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} artificial intelligence litigation; deployment front door; high-impact AI; early disclosure; deployment records; material control
\end{abstract}
